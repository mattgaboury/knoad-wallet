PRE-AUDIT — NOT CLEARED FOR ASSETS OF VALUE

Part I — The idea

What if your wallet was always one step ahead?

Imagine a password that changed itself after every use. Even if someone discovered the old one, it could no longer open the door.

That's the idea behind KNOAD.

Unlike a traditional wallet that keeps relying on the same spending key, KNOAD automatically replaces its active spending key after each included, successfully validated wallet operation. Yesterday's key cannot authorize tomorrow's transactions on that chain.

Keeping yesterday's access out of tomorrow's hands.
If you know, you KNOAD.

The short version

Your crypto wallet is protected by a digital key. In a traditional self-custodial wallet, that spending key usually remains authorized for the life of the address. If someone copies it, the key continues to work until the assets are moved or some other intervention changes control.

KNOAD is built around a different idea: your address should be durable, but the key allowed to spend should not be. After the network includes a qualifying operation, the wallet installs a fresh spending key and stops accepting the previous one. You don't have to generate, remember, or manually swap keys. Your assets do not need to move to a new address just because the daily spending key changes.

KNOAD is self-custodial. A separate 24-word recovery phrase belongs to you, not to Cronosynthesis, and is distinct from the spending key that changes. That separation is central to the design.

Why build this now?

The risks around digital ownership are evolving. AI tools can help criminals create more believable messages, fraudulent sites, synthetic identities, and convincing impersonations at greater speed and scale. The FBI has specifically warned about generative AI being used for financial fraud and social engineering [1].

No wallet can prevent someone from being tricked into approving a malicious transaction. And changing a spending key does not cancel dangerous permissions already granted to another smart contract. But it can remove one persistent weakness: an older, copied spending key no longer remains a permanent means of authorizing transactions after the key has rotated.

Think of this as reducing how long a particular stolen key can remain useful, rather than promising that bad things can never happen. In a world where attempts to compromise people and devices are becoming easier to scale, reducing long-lived access is a practical design choice.

What makes KNOAD different?

Other smart-account wallets can change signers. KNOAD's particular choice is to make that replacement automatic after each qualifying operation, not to claim that no other wallet can ever rotate a key.

What KNOAD does — and what it doesn't

It does: retire old spending keys; keep the account address through rotations; separate recovery authority from ordinary spending; keep signing on the phone; allow a keyless browser relay for website connections; narrow a new token grant to the exact amount and a new NFT grant to one token; tell you when an older permission is still live; let you speed up or cancel a stuck send; and link Discord without handing it a key.

It does not: make your phone immune to malware; reverse transactions an attacker already landed; erase token or NFT allowances that already exist; make a stolen 24-word phrase harmless; remove network fees; scan the chain for old approvals; or provide post-quantum cryptography.

Part II — How it works

1. The system at a glance

KNOAD deliberately separates the part that holds assets from the part that signs today. A smart account controls assets and records which spending key is currently allowed to authorize transactions. The phone creates and holds signing keys, prepares transactions, and presents approval screens. A browser extension relays website sessions to the phone but never holds the wallet's keys. An optional quote service fetches swap and bridge prices; it has no authority to sign or recover the account.

The account and factory hold the assets and do not store the phrase. They have no Cronosynthesis administrator key. The extension can learn the address and cannot sign. The quote service is untrusted; the phone checks the route before it will sign. The account uses ERC-4337 through EntryPoint v0.9 for spending. Recovery is a separate direct onchain transaction. Rotating on one chain does not retire the key on another.

Also on the phone: an ENS name resolves when you send, NFTs held on Ethereum and Base can be read, and biometric unlock opens the device vault without being the 24 words. A site pairs through WalletConnect. The extension only relays that pairing. Pages opened in the phone's browser still sign on the phone's approval sheet, so the browser is not a fifth party. Discord links from Settings through knoad.io. That link cannot sign or recover.

2. The transaction that changes the key

Here is the engine of the wallet. The order matters because a failure in one stage must not be mistaken for success in another.

EventHas the spending key rotated?Did the requested payment succeed?
Signed, but not includedNoNo onchain execution yet
Included; validation failedNoNo
Included; validation succeeded; execution revertedYesNo
Included; validation succeeded; execution succeededYesYes

Saying every successful payment rotates is wrong in the most pedantic, important way: a payment that fails still retires the key.

A small state model

Let K[c,g] be the currently authorized spending key on chain c at generation g. Let N be the next spending key committed by a valid signed operation, and let A be its requested calls.

ValidIncludedOp(c,g,K[c,g],N,A) => K[c,g+1] := N ; generation[c] := g+1 ; then execute(A)

If validation fails or the operation never lands, K[c,g] remains authorized. If execution reverts after valid inclusion, the transition to K[c,g+1] still stands. This is a conceptual state equation, not contract source code.

The account uses one transaction lane. Two conflicting candidate next keys cannot be sent as independent concurrent operations with the same sequence. A pending operation may instead be replaced with a higher-fee version or cancelled using the same previously saved next key. Activity offers both. Settings can prepare the higher-fee replacement after 15 minutes, and the owner still approves it. A key refresh is an operation with an empty execution payload and still rotates; an encoded batch containing zero calls is rejected. Ordinary batches contain at most eight calls, and arbitrary self-calls into the account are restricted.

3. One wallet, separate chain state

A single recovery identity determines a stable smart-account address across the currently listed networks. That shared address is a convenience. It is not the security mechanism.

A separate initial spending key is derived for each supported chain. A newly used chain can receive assets before the account contract is deployed there. Its first key is installed only through a chain-specific activation signed by the original recovery authority.

The phone's registry names Ethereum, Base, Sepolia, and Base Sepolia. Addresses are in Appendix B. The factory, verifier, and account implementation are broadcast on all four, and the runtime code matches this tree. Recovery has been included on Sepolia and Base Sepolia. Ethereum and Base have the bytecode and have not had a recovery included. BNB Smart Chain, Arbitrum, Robinhood Chain, Monad, and ApeChain are registered and not deployed. The phone does not advertise them.

4. Recovering the wallet — without creating a company back door

Your 24-word recovery phrase is the way to regain control if your phone is lost or the active spending key becomes unavailable. It is not the key the phone uses for everyday transactions. KNOAD does not retain the phrase and cannot recover it for you. The words are entered once and then cleared. They are not saved. Secure all networks is optional. Anyone can pay a chain's fee. The spending key cannot take recovery, the verifier, or an upgrade for itself.

On a replacement phone, the user enters the phrase, derives the recovery authority, and can locate the wallet and inspect readable chains without an onchain transaction. The phone prepares chain-specific recovery steps. Each chain must accept its recovery steps in order, and the previous phone can still spend there until that chain actually includes the switch.

Recovery is a direct transaction: a separate account can pay the network fee, even if the KNOAD account has no ETH. A chain that has not yet deployed the account may require special first-use handling. Whoever lands a valid first use or recovery first controls the immediate result; a stolen device can race the owner. This is a real timing risk, not a footnote to hide.

SituationDesigned responseRemaining risk
Phone lost, phrase safeDefault instant recovery installs a new spending key on each recovered chain.Old phone remains capable until each chain accepts its switch.
Phrase stolen, phone safeOwner may optionally configure a recovery delay and current-key veto.The thief can still attempt recovery; without a configured delay, exposure is urgent.
Phrase and phone stolenRecovery can be contested.A delay plus veto can create a deadlock.
Upgrade wantedRecovery-signed, codehash-checked upgrade with a minimum seven-day wait.Malicious recovery authority still matters; not an instant defense.

The default recovery delay is off, because a mandatory wait can make a lost-phone emergency worse. Tightening spending policy can apply immediately with recovery authority; loosening policy is subject to the configured wait and veto process. The everyday spending key cannot replace the recovery authority, change the verifier, or schedule an implementation upgrade.

5. What key rotation does not revoke

Rotation changes your locks. It does not take back the spare key you already handed the neighbor.

  • An approved spender may still move funds without a new KNOAD signature. Disconnecting a website does not remove that, and reading an NFT on the home screen does not either.
  • An ordinary login signature is checked against the current key and generally stops validating after rotation under the documented ERC-7739 flow.
  • An explicit marketplace order grant can remain valid across rotations if it was registered with a named caller and an expiry. Recovery ends the grant by advancing the security epoch. Marketplace acceptance has not yet been demonstrated.

Rotation still does not erase a grant that already lives in a token or NFT contract. The phone says so in Security Center, and again after a rotation, a disconnect, or a new grant. It does not scan those contracts or revoke them for you. A new site grant is narrowed before it is signed. An unlimited ERC-20 approve becomes the exact amount spent in that same transaction, or the phone refuses it. A collection-wide NFT operator grant becomes approval of one token id when that id is in the batch or is the only one of that contract held on the phone, or the phone refuses it. Sending an NFT from the home screen already moves that one token. The account's creation policy still allows a maximum approve, because turning that switch on at creation would change the address. The phone's default does not.

6. The optional trading layer

The wallet's core and the optional commerce features have different dependencies. Creating, receiving, sending, recovering, rotating, managing permissions, and pairing a site do not incur a KNOAD service fee. Normal network gas still applies. Swaps and bridges may carry a clearly displayed KNOAD fee.

A same-chain swap obtains candidate routes through 0x providers; a bridge uses LI.FI. The phone does not blindly trust the quote server. It verifies approved destinations, exact token approvals, intended receiver, token amounts, minimum output where readable, slippage, and route structure. For 0x, the Settler is checked against the provider's onchain registry; Permit2 and permit-style routes are refused. The phone rejects routes it cannot recognize and prechecks optional spending policy.

Commerce ruleCurrent documented behavior
KNOAD fee0.25% default on an optional swap or bridge; capped at 0.30%
No configured recipientKNOAD fee is zero
ComparisonPrefer the higher minimum received; when within 0.1%, lower network fee wins
Quote validityRefetch after 30 seconds or the provider deadline; signed calls must match displayed calls
ApprovalsExact input amount to the intended provider; no unlimited commerce approval

Live swap and bridge submit after those checks. Commerce follows the same exact-approve rule: the allowance is the amount being sold, not an unlimited grant. Some LI.FI destination-side minimum or receiver details are still not fully readable from shared bridge data. Buy / Sell and listings stay inactive. The home screen can read NFTs held on Ethereum and Base. That read is not a trade.

7. If Cronosynthesis disappeared

If Cronosynthesis disappeared, your wallet would be the only thing in the building still working.

KNOAD is designed so the company does not have an administrator key to freeze an account, replace its signer, or upgrade its code. The published factory and recovery design make address computation independent of a KNOAD index. Another bundler can submit a signed operation; a recovery can be submitted by anyone willing to pay its gas.

Linking Discord is a perk, not a key. From Settings, Continue with Discord returns through knoad.io. The phone signs a verification message: no gas, and no token approval. A participating server can then recognize the wallet for a role. The link cannot sign, recover, or upgrade. If it disappeared with the company, the account on chain would be unchanged.

The company-run quote service is an optional dependency: if it vanished, swap and bridge pricing would stop, while basic sends, recovery, and phone-mediated pairing are designed to remain possible.

8. What this design protects against — and what it doesn't

The boundary drawn in Part I is the whole claim. Reduced risk is an old key after its retirement. Still exposed are the current key, a compromised phone, and approvals already granted. Out of scope are a stolen recovery phrase, contract defects, and quantum attacks.

AI makes the social layer of security harder, not easier. KNOAD does not pretend otherwise. A convincing phishing prompt can still trick a person into signing a harmful current transaction. A compromised phone can steal while it controls the current key. A stolen recovery phrase can reclaim the account. Previously granted allowances can outlive all rotations.

Where the design helps is narrower and meaningful: once a chain processes the valid transition, a previous spending key is no longer a standing authorization for future spending on that chain. The phone also narrows the next grant a site can receive: the exact token amount in that transaction, or one NFT, and it refuses the unlimited and collection-wide versions. That's a reduction in persistent exposure, not a substitute for device hygiene, permission management, recovery-phrase protection, contract review, or careful signing.

Quantum reality check

Rotation is many things. Post-quantum is not one of them.

KNOAD presently uses conventional elliptic-curve cryptography (secp256k1 and P-256). Key rotation is not post-quantum cryptography. NIST identifies elliptic-curve systems among the cryptographic systems threatened by a sufficiently capable quantum computer [2]. KNOAD's recovery-controlled verifier upgrade path leaves a potential route for future algorithm changes, but it does not mean a quantum-resistant verifier has been built, audited, or shipped.

Part III — Engineering reference

9. Authorizations, derivation, and address identity

The account's address is independent of the active hot signing key. The account is created through a deterministic factory using the original recovery authority, account index, and hash of the initial creation policy. In conceptual terms:

SALT = H(originalRecoveryAddress, accountIndex, H(initialPolicy))
ACCOUNT = CREATE2(factory, SALT, H(proxyInitCode))

The actual address also depends on the published factory, implementation, and proxy initialization construction. These lines describe the dependency structure, not a byte-for-byte drop-in address calculator. The first active spending key is deliberately absent from the address derivation.

Secret / useDerivation or behavior
Human backup24-word BIP-39 mnemonic with an empty BIP-39 passphrase in this build
Recovery at epoch e, account am/7331'/60'/a'/0'/e'
Initial chain key, account a, chain chainIdm/7331'/60'/a'/1'/<chainId>'
Later spending keysFresh random device-bound keys where hardware support allows; not derived from the mnemonic
Device app passwordUnlocks and encrypts the phone vault, including by biometric unlock where the device offers it. It is not recovery material.

The one-time initial hot key uses secp256k1. Later device-bound keys may use Secure Enclave or StrongBox facilities when supported and may be nonexportable. The phone uses account index zero in the source implementation. A BIP-39 passphrase cannot be added later without changing the derived account; the current UI does not collect one.

Recovery epochs are read from chain state. The device does not need to guess or scan recovery indices. The first-use activation is signed for a specific chain and becomes unusable once that chain's account is deployed. If a compatible account already exists, the factory returns it without replacing its active key.

Generic execute and arbitrary account self-calls are restricted; initialization directly on the implementation reverts. The contract accepts standard ERC-721 and ERC-1155 receiver hooks. The account has one sequence lane; the phone must preserve the saved next key when speeding up or cancelling a pending operation. These are the intended behaviors described in the source paper, not an independent audit finding.

phone:
  nextKey = generate_and_save(expectedGeneration + 1)
  userOp = bind(requestedCalls, nextKey, expectedGeneration, chainId)
  signature = sign(currentKey, userOp)
  submit(userOp, signature)

account validation (if included):
  require(signature matches currentKey)
  require(generation/nonce matches)
  require(nextKey is permitted and nonzero)
  currentKey = nextKey
  generation += 1

execution:
  run requestedCalls   # may revert after rotation

This is illustrative pseudocode. It omits ERC-4337 calldata encoding, signature domains, transaction gas accounting, internal operation binding, and contract-specific revert paths. Consult Spec 1.4 and the source code for authoritative implementation behavior.

14. Continuity and dependency model

A self-custodial architecture does not guarantee uninterrupted RPC access, bundler participation, or correct third-party tooling. An unrecoverable bug in the recovery path could defeat the model. There is no company-wide emergency pause switch.
